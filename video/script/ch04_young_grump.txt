But the army isn't quite done with him. In 1866, General Hazen invites him along to inspect army forts across the Great Plains. Horseback and wagon, Omaha to San Francisco. When they get there, Bierce quits the army for good, and just... stays.

San Francisco is a Gold Rush boomtown, packed with miners, gamblers, con men, and a whole lot of newspapers. And Bierce figures out his real talent: roasting people. Politicians. Preachers. Poets. Anybody.

In 1872, he moves to England for about three years and writes for a London humor magazine called Fun. His first books come out under a fake name: Dod Grile.

Pen names were basically the 1800s version of a burner account. You could say savage things and keep a little distance.

Back in America, he tries something totally different. Around 1880, he heads out to the Dakota Territory, to Deadwood. Yes, that Wild West Deadwood. His job: managing a gold-mining company.

The company fails. That life lasts about a year. Back to the newspapers.
